## Design and invariants

`ha_receipt_watchdog.py`: the monitor retains monitor.lock and the delivery parent retains agent.lock. A fresh subprocess only validates the existing result_ready record against its expected SHA-256, performs public preflight, and submits the identical job ID, lease ID, exit code and output via the existing result API. No claim/authorize/exec/reconcile calls; the child never removes or writes the journal. Only exit codes 0/10/11/12 represent success/transient/reject/bad ACK; unexpected exit is fail-closed. No output or token on the command line; only a local journal digest is passed. The child uses the established agent API, not an alternate endpoint. The parent compares record identity and bytes before each attempt and after ACK, then removes the original journal and fsyncs its directory. It does not erase the journal on timeout, stop, conflicting results, failed preflight, or exhausted attempts. Existing helper validates schema, ownership, permissions, regular file, UUIDs, output length and encoding. Cooperative trusted local root assumed; noncooperating writes and malicious root out of scope.

Limits: total monotonic deadline 45 s per recovery cycle, at most three attempts (13 s wall-clock watchdog per attempt including preflight and result), backoff 1 and 3 s, stop polling 0.1 s. Nominal planned attempts plus backoff 43 s; elapsed time includes process startup and scheduling; overall budget caps subsequent attempts. Parent terminates and reaps only the receipt subprocess on deadline or stop. Stop during backoff also prevents new attempt. These are userspace bounds, not a hard real-time guarantee if process creation, filesystem IO, scheduler, or kill/wait is stalled by kernel. A process killed during a POST may have committed; preserve journal and use existing same-result idempotency on explicit restart. If parent itself crashes the receipt-only process may briefly outlive it; it cannot execute work or erase the journal. Do not promise bounded recovery from kernel hang or hostile local process. Child's actual API functions have socket-level timeouts; the 13 s attempt budget intentionally may cut slow but otherwise valid requests short. Verify on target and tune only after measured evidence.

`ha_monitor_watchdog_candidate.py`: separate candidate using same status and stop files, lock and preflight markers. A valid pending receipt is handled before claim and after child exit; no normal job in the same cycle. Stop with a ready receipt gives STOPPED/RECEIPT_RETAINED; stop with uncertain claimed journal remains BLOCKED. No new claim after a failed ambiguous tick without a journal. Normal child stop remains cooperative; receipt subprocess is the only one cancelled in response to stop. No reset of policy or queue and no automatic reexecution. Explicit restart can begin a new bounded cycle.

## Ordinary child watchdog review

Observed on HA in previous gate: preflight socket 12 s, claim/authorize/result each 20 s socket timeout, remote command maximum 45 s, child wrapper 80 s, polling interval 30 s. Nominal serial budget 12 + 3*20 + 45 = 117 s, exceeding the old wrapper by 37 s. Candidate sets local wrapper watchdog to 135 s (18 s nominal overhead margin). Remote MCP execution cap is unchanged at 45 s; 135 s is not a remote command timeout. This is a conservative design choice, not a proven hard bound on DNS/slow read/IO; ordinary child timeout during a command may leave an orphaned descendant. The prior 300 s job lease evidence is historical and not a universal promise. On wrapper timeout preserve journal and fail closed on uncertain execution; no forced stop or replay of original command. If result_ready is valid, only delivery is permitted. Ordinary stop can wait for the local child watchdog; rollback must confirm both locks, no pending/unknown/running job and no active descendant before starting another monitor. Do not use blanket kill, delete journal, or change permissions as recovery.
